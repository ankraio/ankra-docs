**Ankra AI → Work** (`/next/ai/tickets`) opens **Tickets**, where every piece of Ankra AI's work is a ticket with an owner, a next step and a record of what happened. The Ankra agent picks tickets up, investigates, drafts a plan, has it reviewed where you require it, and waits for a person to approve before it changes anything in production. You follow the work, decide at the gates, and close the loop.

To turn the ticket desk on and work a first ticket end to end, follow [Set up the ticket desk and work tickets with the Ankra agent](/guides/ticket-desk). This page explains how the board works.

## The ticket lifecycle

A ticket has one of these statuses:

| Status | What it means | Who acts |
|--------|---------------|----------|
| **Triage** | Filed, waiting to be picked up | Ankra, or you |
| **Investigating** | The agent is gathering context | Agent |
| **Planning** | The agent is drafting a plan | Agent |
| **Awaiting Review** | A plan is drafted and waits for an independent review | Reviewer |
| **Awaiting Approval** | A plan or a next step waits for a person to approve it | You |
| **Executing** | The approved plan is being carried out | Agent |
| **Verifying** | The agent is checking that the fix took | Agent |
| **Blocked** | The work needs a person to unblock it | You |
| **Done** | Resolved, with a resolution recorded | - |
| **Cancelled** | Closed without the work being done | - |

The board groups tickets by **Lifecycle** unless you choose otherwise, in these lanes:

| Lane | Statuses in it |
|------|----------------|
| **Triage** | Triage |
| **In progress** | Investigating, Planning |
| **Waiting** | Blocked, Awaiting Review, Awaiting Approval |
| **Executing** | Executing |
| **Verifying** | Verifying |
| **Closed** | Done, Cancelled. Shown only while closed tickets are included |

When you close a ticket yourself, the **Resolution** is **Fixed**, **No Action**, **Duplicate**, **Wont Fix** or **Escalated**. Ankra records three more on its own:

| Resolution | Meaning |
|------------|---------|
| `fixed` | The problem was corrected, with evidence recorded on the close: a probe that passed, a run's verification steps, an observation of live state, a link, or a person's word |
| `no_action` | Nothing had to change |
| `duplicate` | Folded into another ticket |
| `wont_fix` | Declined |
| `escalated` | Handed to a person outside the board |
| `unverifiable` | Fixes were applied and reported success, but nothing confirmed them against live state |
| `cleared` | The alert stopped firing after a remediation, without anything confirming the resource is healthy |
| `expired` | The ticket waited at a human gate until it timed out. Nothing was executed |

Closing a ticket as **Fixed** without a **Verification observation** is recorded as your own word, not as a fix the platform verified.

## Where tickets come from

- **Health checks** - problems Ankra AI finds on its own are filed at the severities you choose under **Automatic insight tickets** on the ticket desk. See [AI Insights](/platform/ai-insights).
- **Alert remediation** - when an alert fires and auto-remediation starts, an incident ticket tracks it, and it closes itself when the alert clears. See [Alerts](/guides/alerts).
- **Known Ankra issues** - when an alert comes from a problem in Ankra itself rather than your cluster (an Ankra service failing, Ankra's registry erroring, the cluster agent's credential refused by Ankra), Ankra engineering gets the problem and your board gets a ticket titled `Known Ankra issue: ...`, labelled `known-issue` and `platform-owned`. It is parked at **Blocked**, no agent works it, nothing on the cluster is changed and no question is asked of you. It closes itself as `no_action` when the alert clears, and reopens if the problem returns.
- **Automations** - an automation that reaches an approval gate files a ticket straight into **Awaiting Approval**. See [AI automations](/platform/ai-automations).
- **People and agents** - **New ticket** on **Tickets**, **Create an Ankra AI ticket** in Find, or the `file_ticket` tool from a chat or [MCP](/platform/mcp-tools) session, including [your own agent](/guides/board-own-agent).
- **Chat fixes** - when a person applies a gated write on a cluster through the AI chat or an MCP client (a Stack draft, a scale, a restart), Ankra records it on a receipt ticket labelled `chat-fix`, one per conversation, with every action recorded as a change. Receipts are an audit trail of your own actions, so they stay off the board: its views, its counts and `ticket_list` leave them out. A receipt keeps its number and timeline and opens from a link, and the ticket list API returns receipts when asked with `include_receipts=true`.

Each ticket has a kind - **Incident**, **Insight**, **Gap**, **Automation**, **Request** or **Chore** - and a priority: **Urgent**, **High**, **Medium** or **Low**.

## How the agent works a ticket

A ticket's **Work mode** is **Automatic staffing** or **Manual task**. Ankra assigns an automatic ticket in **Triage** to an agent that may work board tickets, preferring one scoped to the ticket's cluster. With no agent available, the ticket goes to a person instead of waiting silently. A manual task stays with your team: no automatic staffing, mirroring or escalation.

On the ticket desk (**Ticket desk** on **Tickets**) the agents that may work tickets show as one **Ankra agent**, **Available for automatic staffing** or **No eligible agent reported**. The portal cannot change which agents those are. See [Agents that work tickets](/platform/ai-agents#agents-that-work-tickets).

Commenting on a ticket wakes the agent working it, even while the ticket waits at **Awaiting Approval**, so a comment is how you steer it mid-flight. Only a person's comment does this: a comment a model writes, from the AI chat or an MCP client, is recorded on the timeline but does not wake the agent. Write it under **Add context** on the ticket's **Timeline** tab.

### Follow the agent's runs

A ticket's **Work** tab lists its **Agent runs**. **Read run activity** shows what a run did, step by step. **View run details** opens the run's own page, and **Request stop for this run** stops a run that is still going. A link to a ticket with a run in its address opens that run, marked **Selected run · Ankra agent**. See [Follow, steer and stop an agent run](/guides/agent-runs).

## The ticket page

A ticket opens on **Overview**, with **Evidence**, **Plan**, **Work** and **Timeline** beside it. The ticket also shows its **Properties** (**Owner**, **Priority**, **Environment**, **Work mode**, **Labels**) and its **Connected work**.

| Tab | What it shows |
|-----|---------------|
| **Overview** | The **Next step**, or **Needs you** when the ticket waits on a decision, or the **Outcome** once it is closed. Production changes waiting for approval are listed here under **Proposed production changes** |
| **Evidence** | **Evidence and verification**, and the **Recorded changes** |
| **Plan** | The **Proposed plan**, and the buttons to review it |
| **Work** | The **Recorded changes** and the **Agent runs** |
| **Timeline** | Everything that happened on the ticket, and **Add context** for your comment |

### Is it still happening?

An open ticket says in one sentence whether what it is about still holds, for example **Still happening: the alert is firing.**, **No longer happening: the alert stopped firing. You can likely close this ticket.**, or **Not checked again automatically since it was filed. Check before acting.** When the cluster cannot be read, the sentence says why, such as **Cannot check: the cluster was removed from Ankra.** Check before you approve work on a ticket whose problem may be gone.

## Plans, review and approval

The plan is the contract between the agent and you:

1. The agent drafts a plan on the ticket's **Plan** tab. While the ticket is **Planning** and has a plan, **Request independent review** sends it for review.
2. With **Require an independent plan review** ticked and a working reviewer agent (`ankra ai tasks templates hire reviewer`), the plan is reviewed in a pass separate from the work pass. With no reviewer set, the tick does nothing. An agent cannot approve its own work.
3. The ticket waits at **Awaiting Approval**. **Review and decide** opens the review: choose **Approve** or **Request changes** under **Decision**, add a **Review note**, and press **Approve and release work** or **Request changes**. Requesting changes sends the ticket back for the agent to revise the plan with your note.

An agent can also wait at **Awaiting Approval** without a plan, when what it needs is a decision rather than a review. **Review and decide** then approves the next step. With no plan recorded, approving moves the ticket on to execution; it does not approve any production change.

### Production changes

Approving a plan does not widen what the agent may do. Each production change beyond its autonomy still asks on its own, under **Proposed production changes** on the ticket's **Overview** and in [Needs you](/platform/activity-inbox). Each change shows its risk and whether it is **Reversible** or **Rollback not guaranteed**. Tick **I reviewed this specific production change.**, then press **Approve this change** or **Reject change**. Changes proposed together are decided at once, with **Approve all** or **Reject all** followed by their number. Where Ankra cannot show exactly what a change does, only rejecting it is offered on the ticket.

### When the alert clears before the change

An alert can stop firing on its own while a proposed change waits for your approval. Ankra then writes on the ticket's **Timeline** when the alert cleared and that nothing has been applied, and posts the same into the Slack channel that holds the approval card. The change stays a proposal: approve it only if you still want it now that the alert has cleared. Rejecting it changes nothing.

A change applied after its alert had already cleared is not what cleared it, so the ticket never closes as `fixed`. The check that follows the change closes it `unverifiable`, with a note that starts **Resolved before the fix**, and its evidence records when the alert cleared and when the change was applied.

### Answering a choice

When what blocks the ticket is a choice - the agent found more than one way forward and needs you to pick - the ticket opens on the agent's question under **Needs you**, with the options it offered and the one it recommends marked **Recommended**. Nothing is pre-selected. Pick one and add **Additional context** if you want, or choose **Another answer** and write **Your answer**. **Send decision to Ankra** records your answer on the timeline as a comment starting with `Decision:` and hands the ticket back to the agent, which resumes from your answer without asking again. A ticket that leaves **Blocked** for any other reason drops the question.

The same choice is offered everywhere the ticket reaches you:

- **AI chat and MCP** - `ticket_get` shows the pending question under `decision_request`, and the `ticket_decide` tool records your answer ("go with option A on T-8", or "tell T-8 to publish to both for a week"). Ankra records the answer as relayed by you and does not check that a person chose it, so ask the assistant to relay your decision rather than to pick one.
- **CLI** - `ankra tickets get T-8` prints the options with their keys and the recommendation; `ankra tickets decide T-8 --option a` picks one, `--answer "..."` answers with something else, and both together record the option with your note. `ankra tickets list` shows which tickets wait on your decision.
- **Slack and Teams** - the thread reply that announces the block carries the options as buttons (the recommended one highlighted), and so does the escalation card while the ticket still waits. Clicking an option records it as your decision. **Something else** asks you to reply in the thread starting with `Decision:` - that reply is recorded as the answer rather than as a plain comment.
- **API** - `POST /api/v1/org/ai-tickets/{ticket_id}/decision` with `option_key` (one of the offered keys) and/or `answer` (required when no option is chosen). A ticket that is not waiting on a choice answers `409`.

## Recorded changes

Every write an agent or the chat applies on a ticket's behalf is a change on the ticket, listed under **Recorded changes** like a commit log. Each entry names its status, the cluster and Stack it touched, its risk and whether it is reversible, and the operation it created on the cluster's operations ledger, or **No execution is linked to this change.** The verdict follows the operation, not the tool's reply: a change whose apply is still running reads as applying, and one whose apply failed afterwards reads as failed.

The same ledger is on the API at `/api/v1/org/ai-tickets/{ticket_id}/changes`.

## When a person is needed

A ticket needs a person when it is **Awaiting Approval**, **Blocked**, or **Awaiting Review** with a plan the review already approved. The **Needs a human** filter on **Tickets** shows exactly those, and [Needs you](/platform/activity-inbox) lists them with every other decision waiting on a person.

When a ticket becomes blocked or goes stale, Ankra raises an **AI ticket needs a person** notification and records the escalation on the timeline. A plan reaching **Awaiting Approval** raises **Plan for ticket T-&lt;number&gt; awaits your approval**. Both link to the ticket. Route them with [notification routing](/guides/notification-routing).

## Conditions

A condition is one problem that keeps happening to one thing on your clusters. Every ticket Ankra files for an alert or an insight belongs to its condition, so you can see how often the problem came back and how each ticket ended. **Conditions** on **Tickets** opens them (`/next/ai/conditions`).

The list shows each **Condition**, **How often** it was seen, its open **Ticket** and when it was **Last seen**. **Keep coming back** shows only the conditions closed three times in a row without anything being changed, marked **Keeps coming back**. Such a problem needs a lasting fix, not another ticket.

A condition's page shows **What happened** (each **Ticket filed**, **Ticket closed**, **Check run**, **Playbook run** and **Decision**), every ticket filed on it with **How it ended**, and its details.

<CliVersion since="0.74.0" />

```bash
ankra ai conditions list --chronic
ankra ai conditions get <condition-id>
```

## Slack and Teams threads

Choose a Slack or Teams destination on the ticket desk's **Communication** tab and each new automatic ticket opens its own conversation there: timeline events post as replies, and replies in the thread land back on the ticket's timeline, waking the agent. An escalation carries **Assign to me** and, while a plan waits for a verdict, **Approve plan** and **Request changes**; a ticket blocked on a choice carries its options as buttons and **Something else** (see [Answering a choice](#answering-a-choice)). Manual tickets are not mirrored. See [Set up the ticket desk](/guides/ticket-desk#5-choose-where-ticket-conversations-happen).

### Project channels

A [project](/concepts/projects) can have its own Slack channel and Teams conversation for ticket threads. A ticket of that project then opens its thread there, with the same approval, plan and decision buttons. The organisation's channel is the fallback, per provider: a project with only a Slack channel still posts to the organisation's Teams conversation. Set them under **Project channels** on the ticket desk's **Communication** tab, or from the CLI:

```bash
ankra tickets settings projects set core --slack-channel C0123ABCD
ankra tickets settings projects list
```

For example, with projects `core` and `tools`, give `core` the channel `#ankra-core` and `tools` the channel `#ankra-tools`. Add one routing rule per project for its other notifications; see [one channel per project](/guides/notification-routing#example-one-channel-per-project).

<Note>
The Ankra bot's answers in a project channel still come from your default project, and remediation approval cards still go to the organisation's Slack remediation destination.
</Note>

## Mirroring tickets into your own tracker

A **sync connection** mirrors tickets outbound to a Jira project, a Linear team, or your own webhook endpoint: the external issue is created once per ticket, and timeline events follow as comments. Filters decide which kinds and priorities travel. Sync is one-way - status changes arrive as comments, and nothing done in the tracker comes back to the board. The portal has no page for sync connections; set them up with `ankra tickets sync connections create`. See [Sync tickets to Jira, Linear or a webhook](/guides/ticket-sync).

## Views

| Control | What it does |
|---------|--------------|
| **Board** / **List** | The two layouts |
| **Filters** | **Show**: **All open**, **Needs a human**, **Closed tickets** or **My tickets**. Then **Environment scope**, **Ticket type**, **Priority**, **Status**, **Assignee** and **Label** |
| **Display** | **Group by**: **Lifecycle**, **Status**, **Assignee**, **Priority** or **Environment**, and **Order by** |
| **Ticket actions** | **Save view…**, **Select tickets** to change several at once, **Expand all columns** and **Reset to default board** |
| The view picker | **Default board**, and each view you saved |

Saved views are kept in your browser, so they do not follow you to another browser or device.

## Working the board from your own tools

The board is not tied to the portal. Every surface works the same tickets with the same gates:

- **CLI** - [`ankra tickets`](/reference/cli/tickets) lists, reads, comments on, moves and decides tickets from a terminal.
- **Your own agent** - Claude Code, Cursor, Codex or an agent you wrote can pick up tickets over MCP, investigate, draft a plan and carry it out once you approve it. See [Work the AI board with your own agent](/guides/board-own-agent).
- **Slack and Teams** - the ticket's thread, described above.
