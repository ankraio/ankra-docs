**Ankra AI → Autonomy and approvals** (`/next/organisation/ai-autonomy`) is where you decide what Ankra AI may do in your organisation without asking a person first, who approves the rest, and how to stop it. Everything on the page applies to the whole organisation.

<Info>
On a new organisation, auto-remediation is off, and the [default agents](/platform/ai-agents#the-default-team) run only when someone starts them. Nothing changes a cluster on its own until someone turns it on here.
</Info>

## Before you start

- **A role that manages Ankra AI** (the `ai.manage` permission, which owners and admins have) to stop Ankra AI or change the policy. Without it the page reads **Your role can look at these settings but not change them.**
- **A role that manages the organisation** (`organisation.manage`) to change **Finding problems on its own**.
- **A Slack webhook** under **Integrations & alerts → Notification destinations** if fixes are to be approved in Slack. See [Notification routing](/guides/notification-routing).

## Stop Ankra AI

The page opens with two stops, from the gentler to the harder. Both apply to everyone in the organisation straight away.

| Stop | What keeps working | What it does | Undo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pause autonomous actions** | Chat, finding causes and read-only investigation | Ankra AI cannot change anything until a person approves it. It turns auto-remediation off and pauses every AI agent | **Resume autonomous actions** switches back on exactly what the pause switched off, and nothing else |
| **Stop all AI** | Alerts still fire and are recorded, each with a note that the AI analysis was skipped. Pipelines still run, and a new application is still set up, from Ankra's templates | Cancels every running AI conversation and agent run, expires approvals that are waiting, and blocks chat for everyone. An alert that fires while AI is stopped gets no AI analysis, and the scheduled health check waits until AI is resumed. A pull request gets no AI review: its **Ankra AI review** check says that AI is stopped and does not block the merge. A new application's setup skips its AI step and says so. Use it during an incident, not to tighten a policy | **Resume all AI** |

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Portal">
    1. Under **Stop Ankra AI**, press **Pause autonomous actions…** or **Stop all AI…**.
    2. Enter a **Reason**. Everyone in the organisation can read the reason for a pause; only people who manage Ankra AI can read the reason for a stop.
    3. Press **Pause autonomous actions** or **Stop all AI now**.

    **You should see** the pause card read **Paused**, or, after a stop, the stop card read **Stopped** and a banner across the page: **All Ankra AI is stopped**.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="CLI">
    <CliVersion since="0.13.0" />

    ```bash
    ankra ai autonomy pause --reason "investigating a bad deploy"
    ankra ai autonomy stop-all --reason "incident in progress"
    ankra ai autonomy status
    ```

    Undo a pause or a stop in the portal. `ankra ai autonomy resume` and `ankra ai autonomy start-all` are refused with a message that starts **Resuming AI needs a person signed in to the portal**: the CLI signs in with a token, and Ankra makes no token that carries the permission to resume.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="MCP">
    `pause_ai_autonomy` and `resume_ai_autonomy` pause and resume autonomous actions. Stopping all AI has no MCP tool. See [MCP tools](/platform/mcp-tools).
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Fixing alerts automatically

When an alert fires, Ankra AI can find the cause and apply the smallest safe fix. The **Auto-remediation** switch turns this on for every alert in the organisation that Ankra AI is sent. Turning it on asks you to confirm with **Turn auto-remediation on**.

| Field | What it decides | Starting value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **What Ankra AI may do on its own** | **Only look**: finds the cause and reports it, never changes the cluster. **Ask first**: every fix waits for an organisation admin to approve or deny it, on the card the Slack webhook below posts or in **Needs you**. **Fix small things on its own**: low-risk fixes that can be undone run on their own, everything else asks first | **Ask first** |
| **Slack webhook for approvals** | Where the approve and deny buttons are posted. **Manage Slack webhooks** opens **Notification destinations** | **No webhook** |
| **Fixes in one incident** | After this many fixes in one incident, Ankra AI hands over to a person. 1 to 50 | 5 |
| **Wait before the same alert is handled again** | Minutes, for the same alert on the same cluster. 1 to 1440 | 30 |
| **Rules for single fixes** | A rule for one fix that overrides the level: **Run on its own**, **Ask on Slack first** or **Never run**. **Add a rule for** offers `restart_deployment`, `scale_deployment`, `rollback_deployment`, `delete_pod`, `delete_resource`, `cordon_node`, `drain_node` and `apply_manifest` | No rules |
| **Who may approve** | Narrows approving and denying to the organisation admins on the list. The list holds Ankra members (over the API, `approver_user_ids` takes their Ankra user IDs), not Slack member IDs. Whatever it holds, only an organisation admin can approve or deny a fix, in Slack or in Ankra | None: any organisation admin can decide |
| **Only some clusters** | Off: alerts on every cluster are handled. On: only alerts on the **Clusters Ankra AI may fix** | Off |
| **Clusters Ankra AI leaves alone** | Alerts on these clusters are always left to people: no fixes and no approval requests | None |

<Warning>
A rule set to **Run on its own** runs that fix with no approval at all, whatever the level. Set it only for a fix you would approve every time.
</Warning>

Press **Save changes**. **You should see** **Saved. The next alert that fires follows this policy.** Fixes waiting for approval are also listed in [Needs you](/platform/activity-inbox).

<CliVersion since="0.18.0" />

`ankra ai remediation policy` prints the saved policy, and `get_remediation_policy` reads it over MCP. Both are read-only: the policy changes only in the portal. An organisation that never saved a policy reads **Not set up yet. These are the platform's starting values.** in the portal.

To choose which alerts Ankra AI investigates and what it does for each, set up a watch. See [Watch for a problem](/guides/ai-watching).

## Tickets and the board

Where ticket threads are posted and whether plans need an independent review are set on the ticket desk: **Open the ticket desk** under **Tickets and the board**. See [Set up the ticket desk](/guides/ticket-desk).

The portal cannot change which agents work board tickets, or the identity they act as. Use `designate_board_worker` over MCP and `ankra ai board-identity` in the CLI. See [Agents that work tickets](/platform/ai-agents#agents-that-work-tickets).

## Finding problems on its own

This section decides whether Ankra AI checks your clusters' health by itself. What it finds is what auto-remediation and the ticket desk act on.

| Field | What it decides |
| --- | --- |
| **Check cluster health on its own** | On: each problem it finds gets a likely cause, the steps to fix it and questions to ask Ankra AI. Off: nothing below runs, and no ticket or fix is started from a health check |
| **Restarts before a pod counts as unstable** | Leave it empty to use the platform's default |
| **Hours that restarts are counted over** | Only restarts within this many hours count. Leave it empty for the default |
| **Leave out system namespaces** | Skip `kube-system` and the other system namespaces when checking workloads |

**You should see** **Saved. The next health check uses these settings.** Who is told about what Ankra AI finds is set in **Notification routes** (**Open Notification routes**). See [AI Insights](/platform/ai-insights).
