You want to ask Ankra's AI about a failing deploy from the chat tool your team already uses, and have its approvals and findings reach people there. The **Connections** section under **AI** → **Settings** → **Connections** gathers every integration the AI communicates through, in five groups.

## Chat & Notifications

- **Slack** - install the Ankra app into your workspace. See [Connecting Slack](#connecting-slack) below.
- **Microsoft Teams** - connect your tenant and add the Ankra bot to the teams that should reach the AI. See [Connecting Microsoft Teams](#connecting-microsoft-teams) below.

Each connection carries its own **AI mode** - what the AI may do when talked to from that surface. A Slack or Teams connection with no mode of its own follows the organisation-wide **Default AI mode** on the **Workspaces** settings page, which is **Ask** unless an admin changes it. Source-control connections do not follow it: they start with every capability off, see [AI code review](/platform/ai-code-review#ai-mode-ask-or-agent).

### Connecting Slack

Slack takes one Ankra organisation admin to install the app, then each person links their own Ankra account the first time they use it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="An Ankra organisation admin installs the app">
    Under **AI** → **Settings** → **Connections** → **Chat & notifications**, press **Add to Slack** on the **Slack** card (**Add workspace** once one is connected). Slack asks you to authorise the app for your workspace and sends you back to Ankra, which confirms **Slack workspace connected**.

    From a terminal, the admin runs `ankra slack connect` instead. It prints the install address and waits until the install finishes; open the address in any browser and approve it there. The address lasts 10 minutes, as the portal's link does.

    <CliVersion since="0.71.0" />

    ```bash
    ankra slack connect
    ```

    The install link lasts 10 minutes from the moment you press **Add to Slack**. Start the install from Ankra, not from Slack: that is what tells Ankra which organisation's clusters the workspace may see. One Slack workspace links to one Ankra organisation, and installing it from a different organisation revokes every account link in it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Each person links their own Ankra account">
    The first time someone mentions **@Ankra**, sends it a direct message, runs `/ankra`, or presses one of its buttons, Ankra replies to them alone with **Connect Ankra account**. They sign in with their normal Ankra login and confirm the Slack user and workspace shown, and Ankra then answers the question they asked.

    This is per person on purpose: the bot answers with *that* person's Ankra permissions, and their name - never the bot's - is what lands in the audit log.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the AI mode">
    Each workspace row carries an **AI mode** control. **Ask** is read-only, plus low-risk creations. **Agent** lets the AI act, and marks the row **Can act**. A workspace with no mode of its own follows the organisation's **Default AI mode** on the **Workspaces** settings page, which is **Ask** unless an admin changes it. In Ask mode, the buttons that change something - acknowledging an alert, approving a remediation, working a ticket - are refused.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Optionally, choose a home channel">
    See [Ankra's home channel](#ankras-home-channel) below.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The app requests the Slack permissions it needs to answer mentions and direct messages, post replies and cards, run `/ankra`, look up the Slack user who asks, and list channels for the home channel picker. It receives messages that mention it, direct messages, and its App Home - it does not receive the rest of a channel. When it is mentioned inside an existing thread, it reads that thread's replies as context.

### What the bot can do in Slack

- **Ask it anything** - mention **@Ankra** in a channel, message it directly, or open it in Slack's assistant pane. Say `use cluster <name>` to scope the thread to one cluster. An answer in a channel is visible to that channel.
- **`/ankra`** - on its own it links your account. `/ankra status` shows whether you are linked, `/ankra logout` disconnects you, and `/ankra help` shows what it can do.
- **Act on alerts** - alert cards carry **Acknowledge** and **Snooze 1h** buttons, and AI remediation approvals carry **Approve** and **Deny** (**Approve all** and **Deny all** for a batch). A decided card is replaced by its outcome.
- **Approve the AI's writes** - when a turn proposes a change that needs confirmation, the bot posts a **Confirmation needed** card with **Confirm** and **Deny**. Only the person who asked can decide it, and if the cluster changed in the meantime the card shows what moved and offers **Confirm anyway**.
- **Work tickets from the thread** - an escalated ticket's thread carries **Assign to me** and, while a plan is awaiting a verdict, **Approve plan** and **Request changes**. A reply in a ticket's thread is added to the ticket as a comment.

Every button runs as *your* Ankra user through your own linked account, so your role decides what it may do and the audit log records your name.

### Ankra's home channel

The home channel is the Slack channel Ankra posts to when something important happens and no [notification route](/guides/notification-routing) matches: failures, agents going offline, security findings. Only critical and warning notifications fall back to it.

Set it under **AI** → **Settings** → **Workspaces**, on the **Ankra's home** card: pick a channel and press **Save home channel**, or **Clear** to remove it. The card needs a connected Slack workspace, and changing it needs organisation **admin**; everyone else sees the current channel.

### Disconnecting Slack

- **For the whole workspace** - an Ankra organisation admin presses **Disconnect** on the workspace row. Every linked user in that workspace loses their connection and stops receiving notifications. Uninstalling the app from Slack has the same effect.
- **For yourself** - run `/ankra logout`, or press **Disconnect** in the app's Home tab in Slack.

### If Slack does not work

| What you see | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| The bot never answers a mention | Run `/ankra status`. If it says the workspace is not connected to an Ankra organisation yet, an Ankra organisation admin needs to press **Add to Slack** from Ankra. |
| **Install link no longer valid** after authorising | The install took longer than 10 minutes. Press **Add to Slack** in Ankra again - you do not need to remove the app from Slack first. |
| **Almost there** after authorising | The install did not start from Ankra, or linking it to your organisation failed. Retry **Add to Slack** from Ankra. |
| **Link expired** when connecting your account | The connect link is short-lived. Mention the bot again for a fresh one. |
| **Your Ankra connection has expired** | Run `/ankra` to link your account again. |
| **Alert actions are disabled for this workspace** (or AI actions, or ticket actions) | The workspace's AI mode is **Ask**, which refuses buttons that change something. An admin can switch it to **Agent**. |
| **Your Ankra role cannot confirm AI actions in this organisation** | Your Ankra role does not allow the change. Ask an admin, or someone whose role does, to confirm it. |
| The home channel picker says some channels are not listed | The app is missing a Slack permission. Reinstall it from Ankra with **Add to Slack**. |


### Connecting Microsoft Teams

Teams setup involves up to three people. Agree who is who before you start: most delays come from one person waiting on another without knowing it.

| Who | What they do | How often | Ankra login |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Microsoft 365 / Teams administrator** | Allows Ankra AI in your tenant, only needed if it restricts Store apps or requires approval | Once | Not needed |
| **Ankra organisation admin** | Connects the tenant to your Ankra organisation | Once per tenant | Admin role |
| **Everyone else** | Links their own Ankra account | Once per person | Any role |

<Tip>
If your tenant restricts Teams Store apps and your Teams administrator is someone else, send them the [ready-made message](#what-to-send-your-teams-administrator) below. It covers everything they need to do.
</Tip>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Install Ankra AI from the Microsoft Teams Store">
    Ankra AI is published on the [Microsoft Teams Store](https://marketplace.microsoft.com/en-us/product/teams-app/WA200012870):

    1. In Teams, open **Apps**.
    2. Search for **Ankra AI**.
    3. Press **Add**.

    In the Ankra portal, **AI** → **Settings** → **Connections** → **Microsoft Teams** has **Get it from the Teams Store** and **Open in Teams** buttons that take you to the same listing.

    <Note>
    If **Ankra AI** does not appear in the search, or Teams says it needs approval, your tenant restricts Store apps. A Teams administrator allows it once in the **Teams admin center** under **Teams apps** → **Manage apps**: search for **Ankra AI** and press **Allow**. Under **Teams apps** → **Permission policies**, they also check that the people who should use it are allowed to. The [ready-made message](#what-to-send-your-teams-administrator) covers both steps.
    </Note>
  </Step>
  <Step title="An Ankra organisation admin connects the tenant">
    1. In Teams, add **Ankra AI** to a team, group chat, or personal chat.
    2. The bot sends you a welcome card. Press **Connect to Ankra**.
    3. The Ankra portal opens with the claim code already filled in. Press **Connect**.

    That's it. This tells Ankra which organisation's clusters this Teams tenant may see.

    Good to know:

    - **The card arrives in a personal chat.** Whoever redeems the claim code connects the whole tenant, so the code is never posted in a channel. If you add the bot to a team or group chat, it messages you privately and tells the channel who got the code. Anyone else can get it by opening a personal chat with **Ankra AI** and saying hi.
    - **Pasting the code by hand also works.** The card shows the code. In the portal, open **AI** → **Settings** → **Connections** → **Microsoft Teams**, press **I already have a claim code**, and paste it.
    - **The code lasts up to 24 hours.** Asking the bot again returns the same code until it is close to expiring, so a colleague talking to the bot won't invalidate yours.
    - **One tenant, one organisation.** A Microsoft tenant connects to exactly one Ankra organisation, while one organisation can connect several tenants. If a tenant was connected to the wrong organisation, disconnect it there and message the bot for a new code.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Each person links their own Ankra account">
    Message the bot, or mention **@Ankra AI** in a channel, then press **Connect Ankra account** and sign in with your normal Ankra login. You only do this once.

    This is per person on purpose: the bot answers with *your* Ankra permissions, and your name, never the bot's, is what appears in the audit log.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Optionally, set the AI mode">
    The Teams card carries an **AI mode** control. **Ask** is read-only, and **Agent** lets the AI act. With no mode of its own, the tenant follows the organisation's **Default AI mode**, which is **Ask** unless an admin changes it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Optionally, send alerts and pipeline notifications to a Teams channel">
    Once the bot is in a team, alert **Destinations** let you pick a *Team / Channel*, the same way as for Slack. The bot posts the card itself, so you don't need a Power Automate URL. Add the bot to every team whose channels should receive notifications. If a team the bot is already in lists no channels, mention **@Ankra AI** once in that team and reload.

    A destination only receives what a [notification route](/guides/notification-routing) sends it, so add a route for each kind of notification the channel should hear. For pipeline runs, route the [pipeline notification kinds](/guides/ankra-pipelines#what-is-in-place-today). From the CLI:

    ```bash
    ankra alerts destinations channels --provider teams     # find the channel id and tenant id
    ankra alerts destinations create --name "Pipelines in Teams" --type teams \
      --channel-id "19:...@thread.tacv2" --teams-tenant-id <tenant-id>
    ankra alerts routes create --destination-id <destination-id> --organisation-wide \
      --kinds pipeline_run_failed,pipeline_authority_approval_requested,image_gate_blocked,preview_failed
    ankra alerts routes create --destination-id <destination-id> --organisation-wide --severity critical
    ```

    The first route sends pipeline failures, approval requests, blocked images and failed previews; the second adds every critical notification. Add `pipeline_run_succeeded` or `preview_ready` only if the channel wants every green run, since they fire on every run. Without `--organisation-wide`, a route delivers only your current project's notifications. Check where a notification would go with `ankra alerts routes preview --kind pipeline_run_failed`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### What your Teams administrator will want to know

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Does it need admin consent or Graph permissions?">
    No admin consent. Ankra AI is a bot-only app and asks for no tenant-wide Microsoft Graph scope, so allowing it in **Manage apps** and in a permission policy is the whole tenant approval.

    From version 1.1.0, its manifest also declares one resource-specific consent (RSC) permission, `ChannelMessage.Read.Group`. A team owner grants it for their own team when they add the app; it never covers other teams. Whether team owners may grant RSC is a tenant setting; see Microsoft's [resource-specific consent](https://learn.microsoft.com/microsoftteams/resource-specific-consent) guide. If it is off, the bot still installs and works, but it cannot read the thread it is mentioned in. Version 1.0.0 declares no RSC permission. **Manage apps** shows which version your tenant has.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can it read our channel history?">
    Only in teams it was added to, and only to answer. With version 1.1.0, Teams delivers that team's channel messages to the bot, which:

    - Answers only messages that `@mention` it. Everything else in a channel goes unanswered.
    - When mentioned inside a thread, reads that thread so it knows what "this" refers to, for example the alert card the thread started from.
    - Copies a plain reply onto an Ankra board ticket's timeline only when the thread mirrors that ticket, and ignores every other unmentioned message.

    With version 1.0.0, it only receives `@mentions` and direct messages.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What data leaves Microsoft Teams?">
    The messages people send to the bot, the thread a mention was made in (version 1.1.0), and replies in threads that mirror an Ankra board ticket, plus the IDs needed to reply (tenant, conversation and user). These reach the Ankra platform so the assistant can answer, and the conversation is stored against your Ankra organisation. Unmentioned channel messages outside those threads are not stored or exported.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does it need a firewall or network change?">
    No. The bot runs in Ankra's platform and talks outbound to Microsoft's Bot Framework, which relays to Teams. Nothing connects inbound to your network, and there is no IP range to allow-list.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Who does it act as?">
    The person using it. Every answer and button runs with that person's own Ankra permissions and is recorded under their name in your organisation's audit log. Someone who has not linked an account gets a connect prompt instead of an answer. An Ankra organisation admin can also limit what the integration may do with the **AI mode** control on the Teams card (*Ask* for read-only, *Agent* to let it act).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How do we remove it?">
    In the Teams admin center: **Manage apps** → **Ankra AI** → **Block**. If you uploaded the app package instead of using the Store, delete it from your org's catalog. In Ankra: **AI** → **Settings** → **Connections** → **Microsoft Teams** → **Disconnect**, which revokes every linked user's access for that tenant.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### What the bot can do in Teams

The same as [in Slack](#what-the-bot-can-do-in-slack):

- Answer questions. Say `use cluster <name>` to scope a conversation to one cluster.
- Act on alert cards.
- Confirm or deny the AI's proposed changes.
- Work tickets from their thread.

Every button runs as your own linked Ankra user. Instead of `/ankra`, type `help`, `status`, `connect` or `logout` to the bot. `status` (or `whoami`) shows a card with your connection and a **Connect** or **Disconnect** button. `login` works for `connect`, and `signout` or `disconnect` for `logout`. Slack accepts the same words.

## Source Control & AI Review

Connect [GitHub](/integrations/github) (via the Ankra GitHub App), [GitLab](/integrations/gitlab), or [Bitbucket Cloud](/integrations/bitbucket-cloud) so the AI can read repositories, review pull requests, answer `@ankraai` mentions, and power [PR preview environments](/guides/pr-preview-environments).

Every capability is off on a new connection: connecting a provider grants access and nothing else. What each switch does, how per-repository rules override the connection-wide defaults, what a review posts, and when one is skipped are all covered in [AI code review](/platform/ai-code-review).

## MCP Tool Servers

Register your own MCP servers to give Ankra's agent runs extra tools. Each server exposes its tools after a health probe, and every tool needs an explicit **role grant** before runs can call it - registering a server grants nothing by itself. The same steps are available as tools in the [Agent MCP Tool Servers](/platform/mcp-tools#agent-mcp-tool-servers) reference.

This is the opposite direction from Ankra's own [MCP Server](/platform/mcp-server), which lets your MCP client - Claude, Cursor, or your own agent - call Ankra. To have your own agent pick up and work board tickets, see [Work the AI board with your own agent](/guides/board-own-agent).

## Ticket Sync

Mirror [AI board](/platform/ai-board) tickets into a Jira project, a Linear team, or your own webhook endpoint. The external issue is created once per ticket and timeline events follow as comments. Each connection carries its own filters for ticket kind, minimum priority, and whether timeline events are relayed at all. Sync is one-way: edits and comments made in the tracker do not come back to the board.

See [Sync board tickets to Jira, Linear, or a webhook](/guides/ticket-sync) for where each provider value comes from, the webhook payload contract, and troubleshooting.

## Outbound Delivery

- **Destinations** - webhook endpoints that receive platform events, with ready-made templates for Slack, Teams, Discord, and PagerDuty, or a custom JSON payload template. Send a test delivery from the destination's menu to verify the wiring. See the [webhook guide](/guides/webhooks) for payloads and templates.
- **Notification routes** - rules that decide which events (alerts, security findings, agent status, GitOps) go to which destination. Events with no matching route fall back to the home channel. See [Notification routing](/guides/notification-routing) for matching, ordering, and the one-delivery-per-destination guarantee.

### A Slack or Teams channel per project

A route in a [project](/concepts/projects) delivers only that project's notifications; an organisation-wide route delivers every project's and the organisation's own. With projects `core` and `tools` and channels `#ankra-core` and `#ankra-tools`:

1. Create one catch-all route in each project, **This project**, sending to that project's channel.
2. Bind the same channels under [Project channels](/platform/ai-board#project-channels) on the ticket desk's **Communication** tab, so ticket threads with their approval, plan sign-off and decision buttons land there too.

The organisation's own routes and channels stay the fallback in three cases:

- A notification not tied to a project, such as billing, reaches only organisation-wide routes.
- A notification that matches no rule at all goes to the [home channel](/guides/notification-routing#the-home-channel-fallback), and only when it is critical or warning and not an opt-in kind.
- A ticket whose project binds no channel for Slack or Teams opens its thread in the organisation's tickets channel for that provider.

The walkthrough is [one channel per project](/guides/notification-routing#example-one-channel-per-project).

<Note>
The bot's answers in a project channel still come from your default project, and remediation approval cards still go to the organisation's Slack remediation destination.
</Note>

## Permissions

Connecting, disconnecting, and changing destinations, routes, or ticket sync connections requires organisation **admin**. Members can see the ticket sync connection list, but never the credentials, which are write-only and stored in Vault.
