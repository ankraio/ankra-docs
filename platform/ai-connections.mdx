You want to ask Ankra's AI about a failing deploy from the chat tool your team already uses, and have its approvals and findings reach people there. The **Connections** section under **AI** → **Settings** → **Connections** gathers every integration the AI communicates through, in five groups.

## Chat & Notifications

- **Slack** - install the Ankra app into your workspace. See [Connecting Slack](#connecting-slack) below.
- **Microsoft Teams** - connect your tenant and add the Ankra bot to the teams that should reach the AI. See [Connecting Microsoft Teams](#connecting-microsoft-teams) below.

Each connection carries its own **AI mode** - what the AI may do when talked to from that surface. A Slack or Teams connection with no mode of its own follows the organisation-wide **Default AI mode** on the **Workspaces** settings page, which is **Ask** unless an admin changes it. Source-control connections do not follow it: they start with every capability off, see [AI code review](/platform/ai-code-review#ai-mode-ask-or-agent).

### Connecting Slack

Slack takes one Ankra organisation admin to install the app, then each person links their own Ankra account the first time they use it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="An Ankra organisation admin installs the app">
    Under **AI** → **Settings** → **Connections** → **Chat & notifications**, press **Add to Slack** on the **Slack** card (**Add workspace** once one is connected). Slack asks you to authorise the app for your workspace and sends you back to Ankra, which confirms **Slack workspace connected**.

    The install link lasts 10 minutes from the moment you press **Add to Slack**. Start the install from Ankra, not from Slack: that is what tells Ankra which organisation's clusters the workspace may see. One Slack workspace links to one Ankra organisation, and installing it from a different organisation revokes every account link in it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Each person links their own Ankra account">
    The first time someone mentions **@Ankra**, sends it a direct message, runs `/ankra`, or presses one of its buttons, Ankra replies to them alone with **Connect Ankra account**. They sign in with their normal Ankra login and confirm the Slack user and workspace shown, and Ankra then answers the question they asked.

    This is per person on purpose: the bot answers with *that* person's Ankra permissions, and their name - never the bot's - is what lands in the audit log.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the AI mode">
    Each workspace row carries an **AI mode** control. **Ask** is read-only, plus low-risk creations. **Agent** lets the AI act, and marks the row **Can act**. A workspace with no mode of its own follows the organisation's **Default AI mode** on the **Workspaces** settings page, which is **Ask** unless an admin changes it. In Ask mode, the buttons that change something - acknowledging an alert, approving a remediation, working a ticket - are refused.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Optionally, choose a home channel">
    See [Ankra's home channel](#ankras-home-channel) below.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The app requests the Slack permissions it needs to answer mentions and direct messages, post replies and cards, run `/ankra`, look up the Slack user who asks, and list channels for the home channel picker. It receives messages that mention it, direct messages, and its App Home - it does not receive the rest of a channel. When it is mentioned inside an existing thread, it reads that thread's replies as context.

### What the bot can do in Slack

- **Ask it anything** - mention **@Ankra** in a channel, message it directly, or open it in Slack's assistant pane. Say `use cluster <name>` to scope the thread to one cluster. An answer in a channel is visible to that channel.
- **`/ankra`** - on its own it links your account. `/ankra status` shows whether you are linked, `/ankra logout` disconnects you, and `/ankra help` shows what it can do.
- **Act on alerts** - alert cards carry **Acknowledge** and **Snooze 1h** buttons, and AI remediation approvals carry **Approve** and **Deny** (**Approve all** and **Deny all** for a batch). A decided card is replaced by its outcome.
- **Approve the AI's writes** - when a turn proposes a change that needs confirmation, the bot posts a **Confirmation needed** card with **Confirm** and **Deny**. Only the person who asked can decide it, and if the cluster changed in the meantime the card shows what moved and offers **Confirm anyway**.
- **Work tickets from the thread** - an escalated ticket's thread carries **Assign to me** and, while a plan is awaiting a verdict, **Approve plan** and **Request changes**. A reply in a ticket's thread is added to the ticket as a comment.

Every button runs as *your* Ankra user through your own linked account, so your role decides what it may do and the audit log records your name.

### Ankra's home channel

The home channel is the Slack channel Ankra posts to when something important happens and no [notification route](/guides/notification-routing) matches: failures, agents going offline, security findings. Only critical and warning notifications fall back to it.

Set it under **AI** → **Settings** → **Workspaces**, on the **Ankra's home** card: pick a channel and press **Save home channel**, or **Clear** to remove it. The card needs a connected Slack workspace, and changing it needs organisation **admin**; everyone else sees the current channel.

### Disconnecting Slack

- **For the whole workspace** - an Ankra organisation admin presses **Disconnect** on the workspace row. Every linked user in that workspace loses their connection and stops receiving notifications. Uninstalling the app from Slack has the same effect.
- **For yourself** - run `/ankra logout`, or press **Disconnect** in the app's Home tab in Slack.

### If Slack does not work

| What you see | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| The bot never answers a mention | Run `/ankra status`. If it says the workspace is not connected to an Ankra organisation yet, an Ankra organisation admin needs to press **Add to Slack** from Ankra. |
| **Install link no longer valid** after authorising | The install took longer than 10 minutes. Press **Add to Slack** in Ankra again - you do not need to remove the app from Slack first. |
| **Almost there** after authorising | The install did not start from Ankra, or linking it to your organisation failed. Retry **Add to Slack** from Ankra. |
| **Link expired** when connecting your account | The connect link is short-lived. Mention the bot again for a fresh one. |
| **Your Ankra connection has expired** | Run `/ankra` to link your account again. |
| **Alert actions are disabled for this workspace** (or AI actions, or ticket actions) | The workspace's AI mode is **Ask**, which refuses buttons that change something. An admin can switch it to **Agent**. |
| **Your Ankra role cannot confirm AI actions in this organisation** | Your Ankra role does not allow the change. Ask an admin, or someone whose role does, to confirm it. |
| The home channel picker says some channels are not listed | The app is missing a Slack permission. Reinstall it from Ankra with **Add to Slack**. |


### Connecting Microsoft Teams

Rolling Ankra AI out in Teams usually involves three people. Work out who is who before you start - most of the friction in a Teams rollout is one person waiting on another without knowing it.

| Role | What they do | Ankra login needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Microsoft 365 / Teams administrator** | Makes the Ankra AI app available in your Microsoft tenant | No |
| **Ankra organisation admin** | Connects the Teams tenant to your Ankra organisation, once | Yes - admin |
| **Everyone else** | Links their own Ankra account, then just uses it | Yes - any role |

<Steps>
  <Step title="Your Teams administrator makes the app available">
    Ankra AI is not in the Microsoft Teams store, so it is installed from an app package. Your Teams administrator publishes it once for the whole organisation:

    1. Download the package from `https://ankra-gateway.ankra.app/teams/app-package` - a small `.zip` containing the app manifest and icons. **This link needs no Ankra login**, so you can send it straight to an administrator who has no Ankra account. An Ankra admin finds the same package as **Download package** in the portal's guided setup, with **Copy link for a Teams admin** beside it.
    2. In the **Teams admin center**, open **Teams apps** → **Manage apps** → **Upload new app** and select the `.zip`.
    3. Check your app permission policies allow it for the people who should use it (**Teams apps** → **Permission policies**).

    Individuals can install it themselves instead, under **Apps** → **Manage your apps** → **Upload an app**, but only if your tenant permits custom app uploads. Most enterprises disable that, which is why the admin route above is the reliable one.

    See [what your Teams administrator will want to know](#what-your-teams-administrator-will-want-to-know) for the security questions they are likely to ask.
  </Step>
  <Step title="An Ankra organisation admin connects the tenant">
    Add **Ankra AI** to a team, group chat, or personal chat. It greets you with a welcome card carrying a **Connect to Ankra** button - press it and the Ankra portal opens with the claim code already filled in, so you only need to press **Connect**.

    The card also prints the claim code, so you can paste it by hand instead. **AI** → **Settings** → **Connections** → **Microsoft Teams** opens a three-step guided setup - download the package, upload it into Teams, add the bot and connect - and the claim code field is the last step. Press **I already have a claim code** to jump straight to it.

    This step needs Ankra organisation **admin**, and it happens once per Microsoft tenant. It is what tells Ankra which organisation's clusters this Teams workspace may see.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Each person links their own Ankra account">
    Anyone who wants to use the bot messages it, or mentions **@Ankra AI** in a channel, and presses **Connect Ankra account** once. They sign in with their normal Ankra login.

    This is per person on purpose: the bot answers with *that* person's Ankra permissions, and their name - never the bot's - is what lands in the audit log.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Optionally, route alerts to a channel">
    Once the bot is in a team, alert **Destinations** offer a channel picker for Teams the same way they do for Slack: pick *Team / Channel* instead of pasting a Power Automate URL, and the bot posts the Adaptive Card itself. Add the bot to every team whose channels you want to route to.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### What to send your Teams administrator

If your Teams administrator is a different person, this is the whole ask - copy it as is:

```text
We are rolling out Ankra AI, a Kubernetes operations assistant, into Microsoft Teams.
It is a bot-only app: no tab, no Graph API permissions, and nothing to consent to.

Please publish it to our org app catalog:

  1. Download https://ankra-gateway.ankra.app/teams/app-package  (a small .zip - no login needed)
  2. Teams admin center -> Teams apps -> Manage apps -> Upload new app -> select the .zip
  3. Teams apps -> Permission policies -> make sure the policy covering our team allows it

Notes if you are asked:
  - The app requests no Microsoft Graph permissions, so there is no admin consent prompt.
  - The bot cannot read channel history. It only receives messages that @mention it,
    replies in a thread it is already part of, and direct messages sent to it.
  - It opens no inbound connection to our network; it talks outbound to Microsoft's
    Bot Framework only.
  - To remove it later: Manage apps -> Ankra AI -> Block, or delete it from the catalog.
```

### What your Teams administrator will want to know

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Does it need admin consent or Graph permissions?">
    No. Ankra AI is a bot-only app. Its manifest declares no `webApplicationInfo`, no resource-specific consent (RSC), and no device permissions, so there is no Microsoft Graph scope to consent to and no consent prompt for an administrator to approve. Publishing it to the catalog and allowing it in a permission policy is the whole approval.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can it read our channel history?">
    No. A Teams bot only receives messages addressed to it: an `@Ankra AI` mention, a reply inside a thread it is already part of, and direct messages. It cannot read the rest of a channel, and it has no permission that would let it - the resource-specific consent that grants channel-message access is deliberately not requested.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What leaves Microsoft Teams?">
    The text of the messages people send to the bot, and the identifiers needed to reply (tenant, conversation and user ids). These reach the Ankra platform so the assistant can answer, and the conversation is stored against your Ankra organisation. Nothing else is read or exported.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does it need any firewall or network change?">
    No. The bot runs in Ankra's platform and talks outbound to Microsoft's Bot Framework, which relays to Teams. Nothing connects inbound to your network, and there is no IP range to allow-list on your side.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Who can it act as?">
    Only the person clicking. Every answer and every button runs with the linked user's own Ankra permissions and is recorded under their name in your organisation's audit log. Someone who has not linked an account gets a connect prompt rather than a bot-powered answer, and an Ankra organisation admin can cap what the integration may do at all with the **AI mode** control on the Teams card (*Ask* for read-only, *Agent* to let it act).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How do we remove it?">
    In the Teams admin center, **Manage apps** → **Ankra AI** → **Block**, or delete it from the catalog. On the Ankra side, **AI** → **Settings** → **Connections** → **Microsoft Teams** → **Disconnect** revokes every linked user's token for that tenant.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### What the bot can do in Teams

The same as [in Slack](#what-the-bot-can-do-in-slack): answer questions (say `use cluster <name>` to scope a conversation), act on alert cards, confirm or deny the AI's writes, and work tickets from their thread - every button running as your own linked Ankra user. Instead of `/ankra`, type `help`, `status` or `logout` to the bot.

### If something does not work

| What you see | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Invalid Bot** when adding the app | The app package is fine, but the bot registration behind it is not reachable. Contact Ankra support - it is not something you can fix in your tenant. |
| The bot never answers a mention | It only starts a conversation on an `@Ankra AI` mention. In a channel, plain messages are ignored unless they reply in a thread it is already part of. |
| **This Teams workspace is not linked to an Ankra organisation yet** | Step 2 has not been done. An Ankra organisation admin needs to redeem the claim code on the card. |
| The claim code is rejected | Codes expire after 24 hours. Mention the bot again to mint a fresh one. |
| You get a connect prompt every time | Your own account is not linked yet - press **Connect Ankra account** and sign in once. |

For alert cards in Teams **without** the bot, use a webhook destination with the Adaptive Card template instead - see [Microsoft Teams webhooks](/guides/webhooks#microsoft-teams).

## Source Control & AI Review

Connect [GitHub](/integrations/github) (via the Ankra GitHub App), [GitLab](/integrations/gitlab), or [Bitbucket Cloud](/integrations/bitbucket-cloud) so the AI can read repositories, review pull requests, answer `@ankraai` mentions, and power [PR preview environments](/guides/pr-preview-environments).

Every capability is off on a new connection: connecting a provider grants access and nothing else. What each switch does, how per-repository rules override the connection-wide defaults, what a review posts, and when one is skipped are all covered in [AI code review](/platform/ai-code-review).

## MCP Tool Servers

Register your own [MCP](/platform/mcp-server) servers to give agent runs extra tools. Each server exposes its tools after a health probe, and every tool needs an explicit **role grant** before runs can call it - registering a server grants nothing by itself.

## Ticket Sync

Mirror [AI board](/platform/ai-board) tickets into a Jira project, a Linear team, or your own webhook endpoint. The external issue is created once per ticket and timeline events follow as comments. Each connection carries its own filters for ticket kind, minimum priority, and whether timeline events are relayed at all.

See [Sync board tickets to Jira, Linear, or a webhook](/guides/ticket-sync) for where each provider value comes from, the webhook payload contract, and troubleshooting.

## Outbound Delivery

- **Destinations** - webhook endpoints that receive platform events, with ready-made templates for Slack, Teams, Discord, and PagerDuty, or a custom JSON payload template. Send a test delivery from the destination's menu to verify the wiring. See the [webhook guide](/guides/webhooks) for payloads and templates.
- **Notification routes** - rules that decide which events (alerts, security findings, agent status, GitOps) go to which destination. Events with no matching route fall back to the home channel. See [Notification routing](/guides/notification-routing) for matching, ordering, and the one-delivery-per-destination guarantee.

## Permissions

Connecting, disconnecting, and changing destinations, routes, or ticket sync connections requires organisation **admin**. Members can see the ticket sync connection list, but never the credentials, which are write-only and stored in Vault.
