Ankra AI does not know your runbooks, your conventions, or your internal APIs. **Ankra AI → Skills and tools** (`/next/organisation/ai-skills`) is where you teach it: **Skills** are Markdown instructions it reads when the work calls for them, and **Custom tools** are scripts it can run for you in a locked-down sandbox.

<Note>
This page covers what **Ankra AI** uses inside the platform. To teach *your* editor
or assistant (Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot and others) Ankra's conventions, install the
[Agent Skills](/integrations/agent-skills) instead.
</Note>

## Skills

Skills teach Ankra AI how your team works: runbooks, add-on configuration templates, CI/CD pipeline conventions. Ankra ships its own, and people who manage Ankra AI can replace any of them with their own copy or add skills of their own. **Find a skill** searches the list, and **Kind** filters it to **Core**, **CI/CD** or **Add-on** skills.

### How skills resolve

Skills come in two tiers:

- **Ankra's skills** - built in and maintained by Ankra. You can read them but not change them.
- **Your organisation's skills** - added by your organisation.

Your own copy of one of Ankra's skills keeps its name, so Ankra AI reads your copy instead of Ankra's. The list marks it **Replaces Ankra’s**, and **Read…** on it offers **Show what changed from Ankra’s copy**. **Go back to Ankra’s copy…** deletes your copy, and Ankra AI reads Ankra's copy again.

A skill marked **Always in use** is read in every conversation, not only when the work calls for it, and in every check of [your organisation's review rules](/platform/application-ai#which-skills-your-organisation%E2%80%99s-review-rules-use) on repositories where that activity is on.

### Add a skill

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the editor">
    Go to **Ankra AI → Skills and tools** and press **Add skill**. To start from one of Ankra's skills instead, open its options menu and choose **Replace with your own copy…**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in the fields">
    - **Name** - lowercase letters, numbers, dashes and underscores. A skill's name does not change once it is saved.
    - **Add-on charts** (optional) - the charts this skill is about, separated by commas. Ankra AI reads it when one is involved.
    - **Description** - one sentence. Ankra AI reads it to decide when the skill applies.
    - **What the skill teaches** - the instructions, in Markdown, up to 200,000 characters.
    - **Always in use: Ankra AI reads it in every conversation** - switch it on to make Ankra AI read the skill every time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Press **Add skill**, or **Save your copy** for a replacement. The skill is listed under **Skills**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

To change one of your skills later, choose **Change…** in its options menu. **Delete…** removes it; Ankra AI no longer reads it, and this cannot be undone.

### Who can change skills

Everyone who can open the page can read the skills. Adding, changing and deleting them needs a role that manages Ankra AI, such as owner or admin. Ankra's own skills cannot be changed or deleted - only replaced with your own copy.

## Custom tools

Custom tools extend what Ankra AI can do with capabilities specific to your organisation: a security scan, an internal API call, a report generator. Each tool is a script with an input schema; once it is turned on, Ankra AI can call it like any built-in tool, as `custom__<name>`.

### The sandbox model

Each run happens in a locked-down sandbox on a cluster:

- a signed runner image,
- no service account, so no access to the Kubernetes API,
- no network unless you allow it,
- a read-only file system,
- a time limit you set.

The arguments Ankra AI passes arrive as JSON in the `TOOL_INPUT` environment variable. Whatever the script prints is returned to Ankra AI.

### Add a tool

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the editor">
    Go to **Ankra AI → Skills and tools**, scroll to **Custom tools** and press **Add tool**. The **Add a custom tool** dialog opens.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Define the interface">
    Give it a **Name** in snake_case and a **Description** of what it does and when Ankra AI should use it - the description is what Ankra AI reads when deciding to call it, so make it concrete. In **Input schema**, give a JSON Schema object: what Ankra AI must give the tool.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the script">
    Choose where it **Runs in** (**Python 3.12**, **Node.js 20**, **Shell** or **Trivy**), set the **Time limit, in seconds**, and write the **Script**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the guardrails">
    - **Risk** - **Low risk**, **Medium risk** or **High risk**.
    - **Where it may run** - **In a sandbox on any cluster**, or **Only on the clusters I name** with **Clusters it may run on**.
    - **Secret files** - secrets the script reads as files under `/run/agent-secrets/`. Never write a credential into the script.
    - **May reach the internet over HTTPS** - off by default.
    - **Runs without asking: nobody approves each run** - off by default. Leave it off unless you would approve every run.
    - **Turned on: Ankra AI may use it**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Press **Add tool**. The tool is listed under **Custom tools**, with its risk and where it runs.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Every save creates a new version: **Versions…** in the tool's options menu shows them. **Turn off** keeps a tool without offering it to Ankra AI; the list marks it **Turned off**.

### Who can change tools

Adding, changing, turning on or off and deleting custom tools needs a role that manages Ankra AI. Everyone who can open the page can see the tool list and its versions.
