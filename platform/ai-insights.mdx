Most cluster problems start small - a pod restarting, a node filling up - and are noticed only when they page someone. With AI Insights on, Ankra AI checks your clusters' health by itself and gives each problem it finds a likely cause, the steps to fix it and questions to ask Ankra AI. When an alert does fire, a notification carries an AI analysis, so the first person to look starts with an explanation instead of a blank log view.

The health check is off until an owner or admin turns it on for the organisation. The [alert analysis](#alert-triggered-analysis) below works without it.

## Before you start

- Turning the health check on or off for the organisation needs a role that manages the organisation, such as owner or admin. Anyone else sees the switch but cannot change it.
- Each cluster has its own switch, which is on by default. A cluster is checked only when both switches are on.

## Turn on the health check

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Autonomy and approvals">
    Go to **Ankra AI → Autonomy and approvals** (`/next/organisation/ai-autonomy`) and scroll to **Finding problems on its own**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Switch it on">
    Turn on **Check cluster health on its own**. It is off by default.

    The switch now reads on, and the settings below it become active.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tune the check (optional)">
    - **Restarts before a pod counts as unstable** - leave it empty to use the platform's default.
    - **Hours that restarts are counted over** - only restarts within this many hours count. Leave it empty for the default.
    - **Leave out system namespaces** - skips `kube-system` and the other system namespaces when checking workloads.

    Press **Save changes**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Pause or leave out one cluster

A cluster that is noisy for a while, such as one you have just imported, can be paused for a set time instead of turned off.

1. Go to **Infrastructure → Clusters → &lt;your cluster&gt; → Settings → Ankra AI** (`/next/clusters/<cluster-id>/settings/ai`).
2. In **Insights**, press **Pause…**.
3. Under **For how long**, choose **1 hour**, **8 hours**, **1 day**, **1 week**, **Until a date** (any minute up to 90 days ahead), or **Until I turn it on**.
4. Keep the box that starts **Also close the** ticked to close the open insights Ankra AI raised on the cluster and their tickets, or untick it to keep them. It shows only when something is open. A problem that is still there opens again once Ankra AI watches the cluster again.
5. Press **Pause**, or **Turn off** for **Until I turn it on**.

While the cluster is paused, Ankra AI does not check it, so no new insights or tickets come from it. **Insights** shows **Paused** and when it **Starts again**, and the check starts again by itself at that moment. **Resume now** ends the pause early, and **Change…** picks a new length. A cluster turned off stays off until someone presses **Turn on**. You can still ask Ankra AI about the cluster at any time.

Pausing or turning off needs `clusters.write`. It does not change [Alert fixes](/platform/cluster-settings#ankra-ai), which has its own switch on the same page.

If the organisation's switch is off, **Insights** shows **Off for the organisation** whatever the cluster's switch is, with a link to **Ankra AI insights for the organisation**.

**Tickets for this cluster**, beside **Pause…**, opens [Ankra AI → Work](/platform/ai-board) showing only this cluster's tickets.

## Where the problems it finds go

The health check does not have a page of its own in the portal. What it finds reaches people through the places they already work:

| Where | What happens | Set it in |
|---|---|---|
| **Tickets** | A problem at a severity you choose is filed as a ticket on **Ankra AI → Work** | **Ankra AI → Settings → Configure work routing** (the **Ticket desk**) → **Automatic insight tickets** → **File insights at these severities** |
| **Notifications** | A new insight, or a critical one, is sent to your notification destinations | **Integrations & alerts → Notification routes**: route the **AI insight** and **Critical AI insight** events |
| **Auto-remediation** | An enabled [auto-remediation policy](/platform/ai-autonomy) acts on what the health check finds | **Ankra AI → Autonomy and approvals → Fixing alerts automatically** |
| **Ask Ankra and MCP** | Ankra AI reads a cluster's insights when you ask about the cluster, and so can your own tools | Press `⌘J` on the cluster's page and ask what needs attention, or call the `get_cluster_insights` [MCP tool](/platform/mcp-tools) |

With the health check off, nothing is filed or sent from it, and no ticket or fix is started from a health check. You can still ask Ankra AI about a cluster at any time with `⌘J`.

## Alert-triggered analysis

When an [alert](/guides/alerts) fires, Ankra AI writes an analysis from what Ankra has recorded about the alert: the rule that fired, the resource it names and, where there is one, the failed job or operation behind it. The analysis does not read live state from the cluster, so for current pod status, events or logs, ask Ankra AI about the resource. The analysis is attached to the alert's notification.

To read it, open the notification from the bell (`/next/notifications/<id>`). Its **AI analysis** section holds:

- A summary of what happened.
- **Likely cause** - the underlying problem behind the symptom.
- **Recommended actions** - what to do next.
- **Read full analysis** - the complete write-up.

While the analysis is being written, the section reads **Analysis is being prepared. This page updates automatically.** If none was saved, it reads **No saved analysis is available for this notification.**

A firing can also start auto-remediation under the [remediation policy](/platform/ai-autonomy), and the alert itself is listed under **Ankra AI → Watching**.

## Which model reads it

Neither the health check nor the analysis on an alert follows the provider your organisation chose under **Ankra AI → Models and provider**.

- **The analysis on an alert** always runs on Ankra's own access to Groq. It is sent the alert and rule names, the names of the resource and the cluster, the failed job's error messages and the start of its command output, and the Kubernetes events recorded with a failed health check. It is not sent pod logs.
- **The health check** runs on Ankra's own access too: Groq first, and a Claude model if that fails. It counts against your plan's monthly AI allowance. Once the allowance is used up, it runs on your organisation's own key if you added one.

The sub-processor list at [ankra.ai/trust](https://ankra.ai/trust) names OpenRouter (LLM routing), Groq (LLM inference) and Voyage AI (embeddings) for Ankra's AI features. [Models and cost](/platform/ai-provider) covers what your own provider key serves.
