Your account's sign-in settings are on one page: open the account menu (your name in the sidebar), choose **Profile**, then **Sign-in and security** (`/next/account/security`). It has three parts:

| Section | What it holds |
|--------|------------|
| **Ways to sign in** | Every sign-in method that opens your account: email and password, or a Google, GitHub, Microsoft or Apple account. Keep two, so losing one does not lock you out |
| **Two-step sign-in** | Your second steps: passkeys and security keys, and an authenticator app that gives a 6-digit code |
| **Recovery codes** | One-time codes for when you lose your phone and keys. They are available once you have a second step |

## The sign-in page

Opening `platform.ankra.app` while signed out shows **Sign in to Ankra**. Every way in is on that one page:

| On the page | What it does |
|--------|------------|
| **Google**, **Apple**, **GitHub** and **Microsoft** | Each goes straight to that provider, and you come back signed in |
| **Email address**, then **Continue** | Opens the password step for that address |
| A work address on a domain with [single sign-on](/guides/single-sign-on) | Shows **Sign in with single sign-on**, which names the domain. **Continue** opens your organisation's sign-in page |

The way you signed in last time on this browser is listed first and marked **Last used**. It is the way you used, not the one your account began with: an account created with a password and now signed in to with Google shows Google.

**Create an account** opens **Create your Ankra account**, with the same providers and the same address field. A temporary email address cannot be used to sign up.

If a sign-in does not complete, the page that opens says why. **Try again** starts the sign-in again, and **Use another account** signs you out first so that you can sign in as someone else.

After you sign up with an email address and a password, **Verify your email address** asks you to open the link in the email we sent, and moves on by itself once you have. **Use a different address** signs you out and opens **Create your Ankra account**. **Sign out** signs you out.

When your Ankra session has ended and Ankra's sign-in service still has one for this browser, you are signed in again without seeing the page. Signing out ends both, so after a sign-out the page is shown.

### Passwords and resets

**Continue** with an email address opens **Enter your password**, which looks like the first page. A wrong password says so and links to a reset.

**Forgot your password?** opens **Reset your password** with your address filled in. **Send the link** emails you a link, and **Check your inbox** says where it went. **send it again** sends a new one; after that it asks you to wait a minute before the next.

The link opens **Choose a new password**, which lists the password rules and ticks each one as you type. After **Save the password** you are back on the sign-in page; continue with your address, and **Enter your password** says your password is changed.

A link works once, for a limited time. A link that was already used or has expired opens a page that says which, with **Back to sign in**: enter your address there and choose **Forgot your password?** for a new one. Each new link replaces the one before it.

If part of the sign-in page cannot load, these steps show as a plain form, which signs you in, signs you up and resets a password all the same. **Try again** loads the page again.

Signing up with an email address asks you to **Choose a password** with the same rules. `ankra login` opens the same page, with the providers and the address field.

## Add a way to sign in

Under **Ways to sign in**, **Add a way to sign in** lists the methods your account does not have yet. Choose **Google**, **GitHub**, **Microsoft** or **Apple** and Ankra sends you to that provider to sign in. When you come back, the page says **You can now sign in with** and the provider's name, and the method is in the list.

The method your account was made with is marked **Main** and stays. Any other one has **Remove**.

## Add a second step

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add a passkey">
    Under **Two-step sign-in**, select **Add a passkey**. Give it a name under **Name it**, so you can tell your passkeys apart later, then select **Create passkey** and follow your browser's prompt. A passkey uses your fingerprint, face or a security key, and is the quickest second step and the hardest to steal.

    The passkey is listed under **Two-step sign-in** as **Passkey** (or **Security key** for a roaming key), with the date it was added. You can add several.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Or set up an authenticator app">
    On the **Authenticator app** row, select **Set up**. Scan the QR code with an app such as 1Password, Google Authenticator or Authy, select **Next**, enter the **6-digit code from the app**, and select **Turn on**.

    The row now reads "A 6-digit code from the app on your phone." with the date you set it up.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save your recovery codes">
    When Ankra shows **Save your recovery codes**, or when you select **Make codes** on the **Recovery codes** row, the codes appear once. Select **Copy all** or **Download as a file**, store them somewhere safe, tick **I have saved my recovery codes**, and select **Done**.

    The **Recovery codes** row says how many are left. Each works once.
  </Step>
</Steps>

To replace your codes, select **Make new codes** on the **Recovery codes** row. Your current codes stop working the moment the new ones are made.

## Remove a second step

Select **Remove** on the passkey or the authenticator app row, then confirm.

When an organisation you belong to requires a second step, your last one stays: its row has no **Remove**, and the page says "Your organisation requires two-step sign-in, so your last second step stays. Add another to replace it." The platform refuses the removal in the same case with `Your organisation requires MFA, so you cannot remove your last authenticator`. Without that requirement you can remove your last second step.

## Signing in with a second step

After your password or sign-in provider, Ankra shows **Confirm it’s you**. Select **Use a passkey or security key**, or enter the 6-digit code from your authenticator app. Without your device, select **Use a recovery code instead** and enter one of your recovery codes.

Without recovery codes, a lost phone means asking [Ankra support](/platform/support) to get back in. An organisation member with the `members.mfa_reset` permission who also holds every permission you hold can reset your second steps from **Identity & access → People** with **Reset two-step sign-in…** on your row.

## When your organisation requires a second step

An owner or admin can turn on **Require a second step for everyone** under **Identity & access → Sign-in and security → Second step**. This needs the `organisation.manage` permission.

If you have no second step yet, the next page you open shows **Set up two-step sign-in to continue**, even in a session you signed in to before the requirement was turned on, and the portal opens nothing else until you add a passkey or an authenticator app. **Sign out** leaves the screen.

## From the CLI

The CLI hands second-step management to the browser, because passkeys, authenticator set-up and recovery codes all need one:

<CliVersion since="0.9.0" />

```bash
ankra profile auth open   # open your account's sign-in page in the browser
```

A CLI login that meets a second-step challenge completes in the browser and hands control back to the terminal. See the [`ankra profile` reference](/reference/cli/profile).
