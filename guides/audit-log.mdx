Every administrative change in Ankra is recorded in the organisation **Audit Log**. It is the platform's system of record for governance questions: who invited that member, who revealed that Secret, who accepted that CVE as risk, and when.

---

## What is recorded

Each entry captures:

- **Who** - the actor (a member, or a token acting on their behalf)
- **What** - the action and the resource it touched, with structured details of the change
- **When** - the timestamp
- **Where from** - the source IP

Administrative changes across the platform write entries: invitations and role assignments, cluster-group edits, credential changes, cluster lifecycle operations, security disposition changes, and organisation settings edits.

Changes to a cluster agent's settings are recorded with the value they replaced: turning automatic agent upgrades on or off for a cluster (`set_agent_auto_upgrade`), the agent's memory and CPU (`set_agent_resources`) and its pipeline settings (`set_agent_ci_settings`) each record every setting they moved as `changes: {<setting>: {from, to}}`, and the person's reason when they gave one. Turning automatic agent upgrades on or off for the whole organisation is recorded on the `update_organisation` entry, with the earlier value under `previous`.

Kubernetes access is recorded too: cluster access grants and their changes, every kube token minted (including the one behind each generated kubeconfig), every [pod terminal](/platform/pod-terminal) session, whose entry links to a recording of the session, and every reveal of a Kubernetes Secret's values (action `reveal`, resource type `kubernetes_secret`, naming the cluster, namespace, Secret and key names, never the values). Other individual Kubernetes API calls are not in this log; your cluster's own audit policy records those.

Cluster access grants are recorded as `cluster_access_grant_created`, `cluster_access_grant_extended` and `cluster_access_grant_deleted`, and each grant entry records the `authority`: the permission the grant was made under, `kube_access.manage` or, for a [break-glass elevation](/guides/cluster-access#break-glass-elevation) by a caller that holds only the break-glass permission, `kube_access.elevate`. Every change to the [organisation access policy](/guides/cluster-access#organisation-access-policy) is recorded as `cluster_access_policy_updated`.

Work done by Ankra support in a [support session](/security/support-access) is recorded too, under the Ankra support person's own email address, marked as a support session and naming the member it acted as. Refused attempts, the session opening and ending, and changes to consent codes and support access settings each have their own entry (`support_session_started`, `support_session_ended`, `support_session_request_refused`, `support_consent_created`, `support_consent_revoked`, `support_access_changed`).

A deploy that changes an environment's stored deploy options, other than the image tag, is recorded as `change_application_deploy_inputs`. For a [default option](/reference/application-descriptor#default-options) it keeps the value before and after. An option your repository declares is recorded by name only.

When Ankra staff view your organisation from Ankra's internal console outside a support session, that view is recorded as `platform_staff_read` (**Ankra staff viewed this organisation**).

Nobody in your organisation can edit or delete an entry. The platform itself changes a row once after writing it, to add the GitOps commit link when the push the entry describes has completed, and the [retention](#retention) job removes entries older than 365 days. Entries are written for every change regardless of who can view the log.

---

## Who can see it

Viewing the audit log requires the `audit.read` permission, held by **owners** and **admins** by default. It can also be granted to [custom roles](/guides/roles-and-access#permission-matrix), so a compliance or security function can review the log without broader administrative access.

---

## Reading and filtering

Open **Identity & access → Audit log** (`/next/organisation/audit`). Events are listed newest first under **Events**. From there you can:

- **Search** the events by keyword.
- **Filter** with **Who** (one member, or **Anyone**), **Where** (one cluster, or **Anywhere**) and **When** (**Last 7 days**, **Last 30 days**, **Last 90 days** or **Last year**). The page opens on **Last 30 days**.
- **Narrow by area** with the chips **Everything**, **People**, **Sign-in and security**, **Clusters**, **Tokens** and **Settings**.
- **Open an event** to see who acted, the result, what changed from one value to another, and, under **Technical details**, the **Recorded details** as JSON.

The portal has no filter by action type and no custom date range. Use the CLI for those: `ankra org audit-logs list` reads the same trail with `--action`, `--resource-type`, `--since` and `--until`, and `ankra cluster audit-logs <cluster>` reads one cluster's:

<CliVersion since="0.73.0" />

```bash
ankra org audit-logs list --since 24h --actor-type agent --outcome failed
ankra cluster audit-logs prod --since 7d
```

`ankra org audit-logs export` downloads it; see [Exporting from the CLI](/security/audit-export#exporting-from-the-cli).

An event on a cluster names that cluster in the **Where** column. Organisation-level events - sign-ins, invitations, role changes - name no cluster. A cluster you have since deleted keeps its name in the trail, and the **Where** filter lists it as `<name> (deleted)`: the record outlives the cluster, which is what makes the deletion itself auditable.

The filters are carried in the page address, so a narrowed view can be bookmarked or shared as a link.

---

## Per-cluster view

Each cluster carries its own slice of the trail: open **Infrastructure → Clusters → &lt;your cluster&gt; → Technical details → Audit log** (`/next/clusters/<cluster-id>/audit`). It lists every change to that cluster, its Stacks, add-ons and access, and every action an Ankra AI agent takes on it, with the same `audit.read` gate. Its own filters are a search, **Result** (**Any result** or **Did not work**), **When** (from **Last hour** to **Last 30 days**, or **Everything kept**) and the **Who acted** chips **Everyone**, **Ankra AI agents** and **People**. A pod terminal session's event links to **Replay terminal recording**.

Below the list, the sentence "The same events are in the organisation's audit log" links to the organisation log filtered to that cluster over the last year, beside the organisation-level events the cluster page leaves out.

## Standard changes

Entries for changes Ankra makes to a cluster also record which kind of change they were. A change that matches the [standard-change catalogue](/security/change-management#the-standard-change-catalogue) - an add-on upgrade that fixes a published security advisory, a Kubernetes patch upgrade, autoscaling within your limits, a scheduled power action, access expiry - is marked as a pre-approved standard change, with the catalogue entry and the reason it qualified. The cluster's **Audit log** shows it as **Pre-approved SC-01** (or the matching ID) under the action, and **Normal change** otherwise; the full record is under `change_classification` in the entry's recorded details.

Entries without that record are normal changes, or events that are not changes at all. See [Change Management](/security/change-management) for the catalogue and for how to require approval for every change instead.

## Exporting

Click **Export**, then **As CSV, for spreadsheets** or **As JSON, for your SIEM**.

On the organisation log the download honours the filters on screen, including **Where**, and the export rows carry the cluster alongside the actor and action. A cluster's **Audit log** has the same **Export** menu, but its file holds every event on that cluster in the last year that matches the search: it does not narrow by who acted, result or period.

A single export returns up to 100,000 rows and signals truncation when more rows match. See [Audit Export](/security/audit-export) for the full column list and how to get the trail into a SIEM.

---

## Retention

Audit entries are retained for **365 days**, after which older rows are removed by a background retention job. The period is the same for every organisation and has no setting. The log is not hash-chained, so it carries no cryptographic proof that nobody with access to the database altered it.

If your framework requires a longer trail, [export](/security/audit-export) on a schedule into storage you control - the platform is not intended to be your long-term system of record.

---

## Relationship to Git history

Ankra gives you two complementary trails:

- The **audit log** records platform actions - who assigned a role, revealed a Secret, approved an application's launch plan, or changed a disposition.
- **Git history** records desired-state changes as attributed commits - exactly what changed in your cluster definitions and Stacks, and when it was deployed.

Together they answer both "who did what in the platform" and "what actually changed in the cluster". For how commits are produced and attributed, see [GitOps](/concepts/gitops).
