<Note>
When a [stack profile](/concepts/stack-profiles) is public or shared with your organisation, you can do more than deploy it: open it in the profile builder, edit it, and send the result to the owning organisation as a **suggestion**. The owner reviews it and either approves it, which publishes it as the profile's next version, or rejects it with a note. The profile itself stays owner-only throughout: a suggestion never changes anything until an owner approves it.
</Note>

This is how a fix you have proven in production travels back to a shared or public profile without the owner handing out write access, and without exporting YAML and mailing it around. It is also the right lane for the owner's own updates: a builder draft keeps the version's input annotations (titles, descriptions, enum choices, and choices that set other inputs), while editing an IaC export and re-importing it re-detects the inputs from the spec, drops those annotations, and creates a separate new profile instead of a new version.

---

## Propose a change to a profile you do not own

### Before you start

- **You can see the profile.** Public profiles and profiles [shared with your organisation](/concepts/stack-profiles#share-with-specific-organisations) both qualify. A shared profile reads **Shared with you** in **Build & delivery → Stack profiles**.
- **Your organisation does not own it.** On a profile your organisation owns, the button reads **Edit in builder** and you publish directly; the suggestion lane is for everyone else's profiles.

### Edit and submit

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the profile and choose Suggest changes">
    Open the profile from **Build & delivery → Stack profiles** and select **Suggest changes**. The profile builder opens on a draft seeded from the profile's latest version, held in your own organisation. Choosing it again later resumes the same draft: your organisation has one open suggestion draft per profile, so a second click never forks a second copy.

    The builder's header reads **Edit** and the profile's name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Edit like any other draft">
    Use **Edit** to switch between **Resources** and **Launch inputs**, and change add-ons, manifests and inputs exactly as in your own profiles. Save each form before you switch sections; saving a draft does not change any cluster.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Validate the draft">
    Choose **Validate and publish** in **Edit**. Under **Check and publish this version**, select **Validate draft**.

    The section reads **Validation passed.**, or **Resolve these issues before publishing.** with each issue and the resource it concerns.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit the suggestion">
    Enter a **Suggestion title**: one line summarising the change, which is what the owner's review lists. Select **Review suggestion**, then **Submit suggestion** in the **Submit this suggestion?** dialog.

    The builder shows **Suggestion submitted** with **Open profile**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Submitting freezes the draft into the suggestion: the exact spec and inputs at that moment, plus the profile version the draft was seeded from (its base). The draft itself is consumed. A submitted suggestion cannot be edited: to revise one, withdraw it, choose **Suggest changes** again and submit anew.

### While it is open

The portal has no list of a profile's suggestions. Follow yours with the CLI (see [From the CLI](#from-the-cli)):

- `ankra stack-profiles suggestions list <profile-id>` lists the suggestions your organisation can see. On a profile you do not own, that is only your own organisation's; the owner sees everyone's.
- `ankra stack-profiles suggestions withdraw <profile-id> <suggestion-id>` takes an open suggestion out of review, so the owner can no longer approve it. Withdrawing still works if the owner revoked your share in the meantime.
- Each suggestion carries a status: *open*, *approved* (with the version it was published as), *rejected* (with the owner's note, if they left one), or *withdrawn*.

### If the profile moves on while you edit

The draft's base is pinned to the version it was seeded from. You can still submit, and the owner's review shows the drift (see below), but your proposal does not include the newer changes. A suggestion draft cannot be rebased: Ankra refuses with "You can only rebase drafts of profiles owned by your organisation." To start from the newer version instead, discard the draft with `ankra stack-profiles drafts delete <draft-id>` and choose **Suggest changes** again.

### Keep it as your own copy instead

A suggestion draft can also be published as a profile of your own, independent of the original: `ankra stack-profiles drafts publish <draft-id>` publishes it as a new profile in your organisation. Use it when the owner rejects your change, or when your variant diverges too far to be a suggestion. The portal's builder offers only **Review suggestion** on a suggestion draft.

## Review suggestions on a profile you own

The portal has no review screen for suggestions. Owners review them with the CLI:

<Steps>
  <Step title="List the open suggestions">
    `ankra stack-profiles suggestions list <profile-id>` shows each suggestion with its title, status and proposing organisation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read one, and check for upstream drift">
    `ankra stack-profiles suggestions get <profile-id> <suggestion-id>` shows the suggestion's contents and its upstream drift. If you published versions after the suggestion's base, approving publishes the proposed contents as the next version *without* those newer changes. Approving never merges: it publishes exactly what was frozen at submit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approve or reject">
    `approve` publishes the frozen contents as the profile's next version and closes the suggestion in one step. Its changelog defaults to one composed from the suggestion; pass `--changelog` to say more. `reject` closes the suggestion without publishing; the `--note` you leave is shown to the proposing organisation.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Only the owning organisation can approve or reject, and only the proposing organisation can withdraw. Resolving is first-writer-wins: if a colleague approves or rejects a suggestion first, your command reports it was already resolved. Every submit, approve, reject and withdraw is recorded in the [audit log](/guides/audit-log).

## Secrets never travel through a suggestion

The suggestion lane applies the same secret handling as publishing a profile version, in both directions:

- The draft behind **Suggest changes** is seeded from the published version, which stores secret *inputs*, never secret values - so the owner's credentials are not in what you edit.
- At submit, the payload goes through the same redaction as publish: any secret value in the draft is stripped and declared as a required secret input instead. A suggestion that would still carry a plaintext secret is refused outright ("The suggestion contains plaintext secrets and cannot be submitted."), as is a spec carrying a secret placeholder at paths nothing declares as a secret input - so an approved version can never deploy a literal placeholder as a credential.
- The same checks run again at approve, before the frozen payload becomes a published version ("The suggestion contains plaintext secrets and cannot be approved.").

Whoever instantiates the approved version binds their own secret values at deploy time, exactly as with any other profile version.

## Stale drafts on your own profiles

The owner-side twin of upstream drift: you are editing your own profile in the builder and someone else publishes a new version first. Publishing your draft is refused rather than silently overwriting. Under **Check and publish this version**, open **Update the draft's base version**:

- **Keep edits and update base** keeps your draft's definitions and acknowledges the latest published version as its base. Publishing then makes your draft the next version; the changes published in between are not merged in.
- **Replace draft from latest version** replaces the draft and its unsaved work with the latest published version, discarding your edits.

Each asks you to confirm with **Update draft base**. From the CLI, `ankra stack-profiles drafts rebase <draft-id>` keeps the draft's contents, moves its base and reports the upstream changes.

Rebasing applies only to drafts editing a profile your organisation owns. A suggestion draft pins its base instead, and its drift is shown to the owner at review time.

## From the CLI

The same lane is scriptable with the [Ankra CLI](/integrations/ankra-cli) (v0.13.0 or later); the full flag reference is at [ankra stack-profiles](/reference/cli/stack-profiles).

<CliVersion since="0.13.0" />

```bash
# Open a draft on the profile you want to change. On a profile another
# organisation owns, this is a suggestion draft.
ankra stack-profiles drafts create --profile <profile-id>

# Refine what the launch form shows for an input
ankra stack-profiles drafts annotate <draft-id> --parameter replicas \
  --description "Replicas per zone; 3 is the tested production value"

# Preflight the checks submit will run
ankra stack-profiles drafts validate <draft-id>

# Send it for review
ankra stack-profiles drafts submit-suggestion <draft-id> \
  --title "Raise the default replica count to 3"
```

Reviewing as the owner:

```bash
ankra stack-profiles suggestions list <profile-id>
ankra stack-profiles suggestions get <profile-id> <suggestion-id>
ankra stack-profiles suggestions approve <profile-id> <suggestion-id> \
  --changelog "Adopt the community replica defaults"
ankra stack-profiles suggestions reject <profile-id> <suggestion-id> \
  --note "We keep single-replica defaults for the starter tier"

# Proposer side: take an open suggestion back
ankra stack-profiles suggestions withdraw <profile-id> <suggestion-id>
```
